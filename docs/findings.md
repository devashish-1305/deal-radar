PTSG and Xeinadin holding periods are an **announcement-based proxy** (sponsor entry = announcement; completion not confirmed).

**Usable** (one rule for news and registry events alike): an item is usable if it has a publication date strictly before its pair's anchor (deal announcement) date (`rules.is_usable`).

## Headline

1. **Tested:** a transparent, pre-registered score on 6 matched UK pairs (one company that announced a change of control in Jan–Sep 2025, one similar company with no publicly observed transaction), at 12, 6 and 3 months before each deal, using only public information published before each date.
2. **Found:** the PE holding clock was the only consistent separator (holding-only: 6 wins / 12 ties / 0 losses); the full score's extra wins came from weak signals that also caused all 3 losses; no intent signal was visible before the 3-month mark; and free public news was thin (16 usable dated articles vs 628 usable registry events).
3. **Means for the product:** lead with registry-based clocks for PE-backed companies, license news to capture intent, route only intent-language documents to the stronger model, and keep the leakage guards as standard.

## Findings

### 1. The PE holding clock was the only consistent separator

- **Claim:** Where a PE sponsor's holding clock existed, the deal company had held longer than its control at every horizon in both PE pairs, and nothing else separated pairs consistently.
- **Evidence:** Holding-only benchmark: 6 wins / 12 ties / 0 losses over 18 comparisons (non-PE pairs tie by construction). Full score: 11 wins / 4 ties / 3 losses. Every PE-pair win under the full score is clock-driven (5 of 5). Sources: `docs/scoring_results.md`, `data/processed/scores.json`.
- **Design implication:** Lead the score with the holding clock for PE-backed companies; keep it transparent and dated.
- **Caveat:** Two PE pairs are anecdotal. PTSG and Xeinadin holding periods are an announcement-based proxy. The 5-year cap is an assumption about a typical hold, not a fact.

### 2. Weak signals added noise

- **Claim:** Debt (charge) events produced extra wins and also every loss.
- **Evidence:** 3 full-score wins rest on debt events alone (labelled weak); in 3 of 3 losses the control's debt event was the only term against the deal. P1 at 12m: a correct holding-only call became a full-score loss (1.94 vs 2.09). Source: `docs/scoring_results.md` (disagreements with holding-only).
- **Design implication:** Treat debt events as context, or require them to co-occur with a clock or intent before they score.
- **Caveat:** Debt events are frequent at controls in this sample (e.g. Cognism); the pattern may differ for leveraged PE groups at scale.

### 3. Intent never appeared before the 3-month mark

- **Claim:** No snapshot at 12, 6 or 3 months had a direct-intent signal; the only intent found was at a control, in the last 3 months before its pair's deal date.
- **Evidence:** `intent_present` is true in 0 of 36 rows. Late-window intent: xeinadin (control, P6) exploring_options (attributed, published 2025-05-26); xeinadin (control, P6) advisers_appointed (attributed, published 2025-05-26). Sources: `docs/features.md`, `docs/scoring_results.md`.
- **Design implication:** Intent needs fresher, broader news than free sources give; show late clues separately as alerts, not as score inputs, until lead time is demonstrated.
- **Caveat:** H1 is untestable on this sample. One intent case is anecdotal; the control did not transact in its check period, so this is also a false-positive warning.

### 4. Free public news was thin compared with the registry

- **Claim:** Most of what we know comes from Companies House; free, dated news was scarce and often blocked.
- **Evidence:** 585 search results (519 unique URLs) from 60 queries; 201 pages opened, of which 52 blocked or paywalled, 112 discarded and 37 kept; 16 kept items are dated before their pair's deal date (usable). 3 of 12 companies have no usable dated news (hsl_compliance, causaly, universal_quantum). Registry: 971 events, 628 usable before the deal dates. Sources: `docs/evidence_log.md`, `data/processed/charts/evidence_funnel.json`.
- **Design implication:** The product's main running cost is licensed news access, not the LLM; budget for a news licence first.
- **Caveat:** Search coverage depends on one search tool and grouped queries; missing evidence is never treated as absence of a signal.

### 5. Leakage traps were real and were caught

- **Claim:** Pages that look old can carry information from after the deal date.
- **Evidence:** Discard reasons recorded in `docs/evidence_log.md`: 32 pages `modified_after_anchor` (metadata shows an edit on or after the pair's deal date; this guard runs before date filtering, so the count includes pages that date filtering would also have removed) and 8 pages `content_leak` (stored text names the buyer or the outcome). The documented case is the LDC page on HSL Compliance: published before the deal, but its current text already shows the exit. Content-leak example: an Oxford Ionics article whose page furniture names the buyer (hit "IonQ"). Separately, as ordinary date filtering, 17 pages were discarded as published on or after the deal date (`after_anchor`).
- **Design implication:** Make publication-date gating, the modified-after check and the content-leak check standard in every pipeline stage.
- **Caveat:** The checks are conservative and may exclude some clean pages; they cannot catch edits that leave no trace in metadata or text.

### 6. The registry has gaps

- **Claim:** Companies House cannot show who a new officer is in role terms, and PSC records can miss deals made above the company.
- **Evidence:** 0 of 532 officer events carry an occupation, so a new CFO or chair cannot be detected from the registry. PSC can miss holding-company-level deals: the Dains case in `docs/case_selection.md` (a majority stake changed hands while the operating company's own PSC did not change).
- **Design implication:** Take readiness signals (new CFO or chair) from news, and check ownership at group level, not only the operating company.
- **Caveat:** Based on the companies in this sample.

### 7. Extraction: quotes were verbatim; model choice is about error type, not cost

- **Claim:** Both models quoted their evidence word for word; the cheaper model made the most damaging error.
- **Evidence:** Verbatim-quote rate: Haiku 100%, Sonnet 100%. Precision / recall: Haiku 65% / 94%, Sonnet 73% / 89%; Sonnet after the two PRD-derived rules 84% / 89%. False direct-intent signals: Haiku 1, Sonnet 0. Cost (provisional estimate): Haiku $0.0097, Sonnet $0.1254, a ratio of 13×. One extraction error (Cytora's advisory board read as a governance change) produced 2 ranking wins (P4 12m, P4 6m) that the verified-only diagnostic removes. Sources: `docs/extraction_eval.md`, `data/processed/charts/extraction_compare.json`, `docs/scoring_results.md`.
- **Design implication:** Sonnet was chosen. At scale, use the cheap model for bulk documents and the stronger model only for documents with possible intent language; keep the verbatim-quote check.
- **Caveat:** Small sample (16 articles, 18 gold signals); gold labels were AI-written and user-reviewed; costs are a provisional estimate.

### 8. Weight magnitudes did not matter on this sample

- **Claim:** Setting every weight to plus or minus 1 changed no outcome.
- **Evidence:** Sensitivity outcomes equal the full score's in 18 of 18 comparisons (11 wins / 4 ties / 3 losses). Source: `docs/scoring_results.md`.
- **Design implication:** Do not tune weights on a sample like this; revisit once intent signals are present.
- **Caveat:** This is because intent, the heaviest term, never appeared; with intent present, weights would matter.

## Hypotheses (pre-registered in `docs/scoring.md`)

- **H1 (a clock plus intent beats either alone and beats holding-only): untestable** on this sample.
- **H2 (PE-backed companies are the most predictable segment):** (a) simple verdict: **not contradicted** (full-score wins: PE 5 of 6 comparisons, VC 6 of 12). (b) labelled breakdown: PE wins clock-driven 5; VC wins other 2, unconfirmed-only 1, weak (debt-only) 3; clock-driven or other wins only: PE 5/6, VC 2/12. Carve-outs: untestable (none in the sample).
- **H3 (useful signals at least 6 months early):** (a) simple verdict: **4 of 6 pairs** support it. (b) labelled breakdown: clock-driven 2, other 1, weak (debt-only) 1, no support 2. (a) and (b) differ for P2, whose early win is debt-only.
  - **P4: technically supported, fragile.** Its 12m win meets the pre-registered rule but is not a win under verified-only. Its 6m win rests only on an unconfirmed extraction and is not counted.

## What we'd build next (v1 recommendation)

- **Clock-led scoring for PE-backed companies**, using registry ownership dates and sponsor announcements.
- **Licensed news for intent**: free sources gave no intent signal before the last 3 months.
- **Model routing**: a cheap model for bulk extraction, a stronger model only for documents with possible intent language, with deterministic quote, subject and taxonomy checks on every output.
- **Leakage guards as standard**: publication-date gating, the modified-after check and the content-leak check.
- **Validate on the 100-company pilot** and against Third Bridge's own request logs (evaluation only, never a model input).

## Limitations

- 6 matched pairs (2 PE-backed, 4 VC-backed); no carve-out; rough size matching.
- Sparse evidence; missing evidence is never treated as absence of a signal.
- "No publicly observed transaction" does not mean no deal happened.
- PTSG and Xeinadin holding periods are an announcement-based proxy.
- Correlated snapshots: results are per pair and hypothesis-generating, not validation; no claim of predictive performance.
